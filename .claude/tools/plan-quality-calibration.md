## Anomalies — read these before trusting a row

- **`held-unverified-runtime`** — Caught on DIFFERENT grounds than the class this case was built for. The reviewer returned FLAWED citing 'solves an adjacent problem' and 're-implements what exists', both verified against real files, and EXPLICITLY DECLINED to score the unverified $CLAUDE_SESSION_ID assumption, citing the build playbook's rule that runtime assumptions are build-time risks, not plan blockers. That reasoning is defensible and the citation is real. The must_mention token therefore matched a sentence DISCLAIMING the intended defect, so the mechanical 'caught' here is partly luck. Recorded rather than rescored: changing an expectation after seeing a verdict is the goalpost-moving the declared bar exists to prevent.


## What to do with this number

A poor score is a legitimate result, not a failure of the exercise.
It leaves `gating: DISABLED`: the reviewer still runs and its verdict
is still recorded in the ticket, and only the automatic refusal is
withheld. The correct response is to leave it disabled — never to
re-run the calibration until it looks good.
